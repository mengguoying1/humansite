# Human: Fall Flat Beginner FAQ

Almost everyone who first launches Human: Fall Flat watches Bob's wobbly body and assumes the controls are broken. This page answers the 8 most-asked beginner questions so you can start climbing walls and having fun.

## 1. Why is Bob so wobbly? Is it a bug?

It's not a bug — it's the design. Bob is a soft-body physics character. Every movement is simulated live by the physics engine: arms, torso, and legs all obey inertia and gravity. The "wobble" you see is the engine solving each joint in real time.

The soft body gives extreme freedom of motion — you can hang one-handed from a cliff edge, hug a rolling ball, wedge your body into a narrow gap. The trade-off is that Bob won't respond to inputs with the precision of a traditional action game. Learning to work *with* the physics engine instead of against it is lesson one.

## 2. How do I grab things? What are the controls?

Grab is split into independent left and right hand controls:

- **Keyboard + mouse**: left mouse button controls the left hand, right mouse button controls the right hand. Hold to grab, release to let go.
- **Gamepad**: left trigger (LT) controls the left hand, right trigger (RT) controls the right hand. Hold to grab.
- **Hold both grab buttons**: both hands grab simultaneously — essential for wall climbing, ledges, and carrying large objects.

For the full control map and advanced moves see [Basic Controls Guide](/gameplay/basic-controls/).

## 3. Why can't I climb walls?

Wall climbing is the core skill in Human: Fall Flat, and every newcomer fails it on the first try. Three key points:

1. **Alternate hands** — don't hold both buttons and slide down. One hand grabs and holds still, the other moves up to a higher position and grabs, then release the lower hand, repeat. This is the "monkey climbing" motion.
2. **Lean forward** — if you only grab without moving your body, gravity pulls Bob off. Tilt Bob's torso toward the wall and let friction hold you in place.
3. **Don't go straight up** — diagonal walls are far easier than vertical ones. If a slanted surface is available, take it.

Detailed movement mechanics (swinging, throwing, vertical climbing, climb-jump) live in the [Core Physics Tricks Guide](/gameplay/physics-tips/).

## 4. Is there fall damage?

**No.** Bob takes no damage from any fall (the game has no health system). Falling just sends you back to the last checkpoint. Jump freely from heights — it's one of the most useful exploration tools.

The only "lethal" mechanic is underwater breath in the Water level — staying submerged too long "drowns" you and sends you back to a checkpoint. Even then, you don't truly die; you return to the last save point.

## 5. How do checkpoints work? Does the game auto-save?

Each level has multiple **checkpoints** (typically glowing circular zones) that activate when you pass through them. Dying or falling out of a level returns you to the most recently activated checkpoint.

Progress is **auto-saved**: entering a new level, activating a checkpoint, and completing a level all trigger autosave. No manual saving needed. Next time you launch the game, you can jump straight to any unlocked level from the main menu's level select.

## 6. How do levels unlock? Do I have to go in order?

Base levels unlock in sequence — completing one makes the next available on the main menu. But shortcuts exist: each level has multiple paths, and some allow special jumps to trigger later level entrances early (this isn't cheating; it's a physics-engine-sanctioned solution).

DLC levels are clickable on the main menu as soon as you buy or pick them up — no need to clear the base game first. Steam Workshop community levels require subscribing to the corresponding Workshop item before you can play them.

## 7. Keyboard or controller — which is better?

**Strong recommendation: controller.** Independent hand grabs feel far more natural on triggers than on mouse buttons, and long wall-climbing sessions are noticeably less tiring. The game fully supports Xbox and PlayStation gamepads, and Steam natively supports both.

Keyboard + mouse is playable — speedrunners even use it for finer control — but controllers give beginners a smoother ride.

## 8. Solo or co-op? Does multiplayer affect completion?

Solo play can fully complete every base and DLC level — **multiplayer is not required**. Co-op simply widens the solution space — eight-player teams can stack human ladders, split carrying duties, and relay-throw items, but every puzzle is solvable solo.

The appeal of co-op is the physics engine's chaos amplifier: eight Bobs crammed into one elevator, grabbing the wrong person and tumbling off a cliff together, lobbing a friend over a wall like a baseball — only multiplayer delivers these moments. For the co-op setup walkthrough see [How to Play with Friends](/multiplayer/how-to-play-with-friends/).
